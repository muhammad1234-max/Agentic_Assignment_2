WORLD HEALTH ORGANIZATION (WHO)
TECHNICAL FACT SHEET: HEATWAVES AND HEALTH - RISK REDUCTION AND ADVICE FOR THE PUBLIC

1. Heat Exposure and Human Physiology:
Heatwaves can trigger severe thermal stress, exacerbating pre-existing chronic conditions (cerebrovascular, cardiovascular, and respiratory diseases) and inducing acute illnesses including heat cramps, heat syncope, heat exhaustion, and fatal heatstroke.
- Rapid elevations in ambient heat compromise the body's thermoregulation, causing increased heart rate, blood viscosity, dehydration, and potential organ failure.

2. Five Core WHO Recommendations for Staying Safe:
- Keep your home cool: Monitor indoor temperatures. Close curtains and shutters on sun-facing windows during daylight hours. Open windows at night and early morning when ambient outdoor temperature drops.
- Keep out of the heat: Move to the coolest room in the house, particularly at night. If indoor conditions exceed 32°C and cannot be cooled, spend 2 to 3 hours in an air-conditioned public building.
- Keep the body cool and hydrated: Take cool showers or foot baths. Regularly drink water throughout the day. Avoid sugary drinks, alcohol, and excessive caffeine as they accelerate fluid loss.
- Protect vulnerable individuals: Regularly check on elderly relatives, isolated neighbours, infants, and bedridden individuals at least twice daily.
- Recognize red-flag medical signs: Muscle spasms, acute dizziness, confusion, hot dry skin, or loss of consciousness require urgent emergency paramedic care.
