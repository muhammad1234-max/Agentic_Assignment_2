SINDH ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION AGENCY (SEPA) & WHO GUIDELINES
PUBLIC HEALTH ADVISORY: COMBINED HEALTH RISKS OF AIR POLLUTION (PM2.5 / PM10) AND HIGH TEMPERATURES IN URBAN KARACHI

1. Particulate Matter Overview and Standards:
- PM2.5 (Fine Particulate Matter <= 2.5 micrometers): Deeply penetrable into the pulmonary alveoli and enters the bloodstream. Sources in Karachi include vehicular combustion, industrial emissions (Korangi, SITE, Port Qasim), open waste burning, and construction dust.
- PM10 (Coarse Inhalable Particles <= 10 micrometers): Irritates the upper respiratory tract, eyes, and throat. Heavy levels exacerbated by sea spray particles, windborne soil, and desert dust.
- WHO Global Air Quality Guidelines: 24-hour mean PM2.5 threshold is 15 ug/m3; PM10 threshold is 45 ug/m3.
- Sindh Environmental Quality Standards (SEQS): 24-hour PM2.5 limit is 35 ug/m3; PM10 limit is 150 ug/m3.

2. Synergistic Impact of Air Pollution and Extreme Heat:
When high ambient temperature coincides with elevated PM2.5 and PM10 concentrations, the physiological cardiovascular and respiratory strain compounds multiplicatively.
- High thermal stress causes increased inhalation rates and dilated peripheral blood vessels, accelerating the uptake of ultrafine particulate matter into the systemic circulation.
- Asthma patients, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) sufferers, young children, and elderly citizens experience acute bronchial constriction, reduced lung function, and heightened risk of cardiac arrest.

3. Actionable Precautions for Karachi Citizens:
- If PM2.5 exceeds 50 ug/m3 or air quality index indicates unhealthy air, sensitive individuals should avoid strenuous outdoor activities, jogging, and outdoor sports, especially along heavily trafficked arterial roads (e.g., Shahrah-e-Faisal, Mauripur Road).
- Evening seaside jogging (Clifton, Sea View): Even near the coastline, localized vehicular traffic and coastal inversion layers can trap particulates. Vulnerable individuals should exercise indoors during high AQI or stagnant wind conditions.
- Wear certified N95 or well-fitted particulate masks when traveling through industrial or high-traffic corridors during haze episodes.
- Keep inhalers and prescribed respiratory medications readily accessible.
